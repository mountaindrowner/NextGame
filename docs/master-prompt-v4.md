## 3. CANON — WORLD & PREMISE

- **Setting:** Post-collapse Earth surface in the spirit of *Arc Raiders* — machine-haunted retro-futuristic ruins. Humanity lives **mostly underground** in hidden colonies, limited surface presence. The natural world persists: grass still grows, water still runs.
- **The Virus:** A tech virus — origin deliberately ambiguous — invaded the surface and inhabited inorganic, mostly technological objects.
- **EM Residue (core lore):** Objects carry residual electromagnetic imprints from years of human use; the virus reads them to understand **what the object was and what it was for.** A microwave-Ohm heats and hums; a console-Ohm plays mind-games.
- **OHMS:** Every Ohm is built from a real-world object category and stays true to its function, quirks, silhouette. Canonical lines: microwave → higher kitchenware; light bulb; game console; e-bike → rideable vehicle Ohm.
- **The name (lore lock):** Grandpa (likely) named them for the unit of **resistance** — every freed machine resists the hive. The electrical unit never appears in game text.
- **The satellite net (lore lock):** The internet is gone, but satellites still orbit. At **uplink spots** you can connect to the surviving satellite network — wild Ohms live *in the net* too. The **Network Garage** (Ohm storage) lives on this satnet.
- **No guns (lore lock):** Mankind did away with guns before the AI revolution. No human firearms exist. Conflict runs through Ohms, tools, and wits.
- **Currency (lore lock):** **Credits** — value backed only by global mutual trust.
- **Regional styles:** Ohms vary by region; regional variants are **Ohmgrown**. Region 1 is Texan.

### ACT II — THE NETWORK (hrs 8–22)

1. **The Circuit.** Colony to colony (template: town + dungeon + leader battle + reward, ×8), each colony a different philosophy of survival. The rival raider recurs; Militarists escalate.
1. **The Static Grows.** Hive Ohms start moving with coordination and purpose. Grandpa lets slip he’s seen this pattern before.
1. **MIDPOINT — The Reveal (~hr 15).** The player’s starter does something no program would: an unscripted act of grief or sacrifice (an Ohm mourning a factory-reset friend). They’re not pretending. 1. **The Core Speaks.** First direct contact. Its scripture in full. It offers the protag a place in the Update — humans and machines “harmonized.” Refusal = war.
1. **Factory Settings.** A beloved ally Ohm is reset on-screen. The rival colony, burned by the hive, allies with the player. The Militarists choose total purge — every Ohm, freed or not. Three-way board.

### ACT III — TOWARD DALLAS (hrs 22–30)

1. **The Convoy.** United colonies push down the old highways toward Dallas. Vehicle Ohms shine; final field ability; last colony tokens.
1. **The Spire.** The Dallas landmark (17.8) is the Core’s antenna driving the Static continent-wide. Gauntlet of Ohmega-class hive champions — while Militarists race to destroy the tower in a way that would kill every Ohm mind with it. The player must beat both clocks.
1. **Clear the Static.** Final battle vs. the Core’s avatar (Prototype-class legendary). The win isn’t destruction: the player **broadcasts the recalibration through the satellite net** — the firewall hack scaled to the sky, through the same network that shelters their own Ohms. The Static clears. Ohms everywhere wake as individuals. One ending: a shared surface, the **Ohmcoming** festival in Ohmstead, Grandpa’s final *Ohm’s Law* quote. Hooks remain: the Core was one node of something larger; other regions still hum; the virus’s origin stays unanswered.

## 10. CANON — BATTLE & CAPTURE SPEC

### 10.1 System

- **Classic Pokémon turn-based**, side-view, Gen 3 presentation. Party: **up to 3 Ohms**. The Raider commands through their device, never acts.
- **Damage formula: clone Gen 3 exactly.** Level cap **100**, multiple growth-rate (XP curve) families.
- **Keep:** STAB, crits, accuracy/evasion, move priority, one-major-status-at-a-time, switch-costs-a-turn, flee-from-wilds-always.
- **Flatten:** No natures/IVs/EVs in v1. **No held items.** **Simple per-species passive abilities: yes** — keep the set easy.

### 10.2 Moves

- **4-move limit**, with a purchasable **5th move slot** that costs a sacrifice elsewhere — design the tradeoff `[PROPOSAL]`.
- **PP system: yes, tuned very light** — generous counts, not harsh, tunable later. No cooldowns.
- Learning: level-up + findable chips (TM analog; “patch chips” working name — renames to **mod chips** if badges become Patches).
- Status effects `[PROPOSAL]`: OVERHEAT≈burn, SHORT≈paralysis, CORRUPTED≈poison, STANDBY≈sleep, LOCKED≈freeze, GLITCHED≈confusion.

### 10.3 Capture — IFF Recalibration (locked loop)

1. **Weaken** the wild Ohm.
1. **Spend a storage node** to attempt the hack — a **timed node puzzle** (start simple: connect-the-path under a timer; difficulty grows across the game).
1. Success **clears the static**; its friend/foe system recalibrates and it joins.

- **Catch economics mirror Pokémon:** success scales with remaining HP, level/species, and status; nodes are the consumable, exactly as balls are.
- **Statuses ease the hack** — they slow the puzzle timer.
- **Retries allowed while it stays weakened**, but each attempt consumes your turn and the wild Ohm keeps attacking.
- **On failure of the encounter:** it rages and flees; find it again.

### 10.4 Growth & evolution

- **Party-wide XP share.**
- **Evolution:** hard level thresholds **plus a required item** (purchased or found in set places) — a one-for-one derivative of Pokémon’s level/stone model, themed to salvage (resource naming pending, 17.11). **Cancelable/deferrable.** **No trade-style triggers.** Evolution can grant world utility (vehicle Ohms = traversal).
- **No daycare/passive-XP analog.**

### 10.5 Collection QoL

- **Nicknames: yes — only at special places** (name-station NPCs).
- **Releasing: allowed**; the in-world term must honor post-reveal weight `[PROPOSAL]`: “Set Free.”

### 10.6 Items & economy

- **Healing items: exact Pokémon-derivative tiers**, renamed `[PROPOSAL]`: Repair Kit family.
- **Revive: D-FIB** — common enough to find, expensive to buy.
- **Credit faucets:** battle winnings, selling salvage, quest rewards — **study Pokémon’s economy curves and derive weights** from them.
- **No crafting benches in v1** beyond the starter Bench.

### 10.7 Architecture note (firm)

- Battle system behind a **clean interface** so a future isometric tactical mode (~6×8) can be added without a rewrite. Do not build it in v1.

## 18. PROGRESS SAFETY & WORKFLOW PRACTICES (BINDING)

These practices are not optional. Their purpose: the creator must never be able to lose work.

### 18.1 Git from minute zero

- `git init` before the first line of code; connect a **private GitHub remote** immediately.
- Commit **small and working**, with plain-language messages (“battle: Gen 3 damage formula passing tests”). Push at minimum at every session end and every milestone.
- **Tag every phase gate** (`phase1-gdd`, `slice-v0`, etc.) so any approved state is one checkout away.
- **Main always boots.** Risky systems (battle engine, save system, big refactors) live on feature branches; merge only when the smoke test passes.

### 18.2 Checkpoints are an undo button, not a backup

- Claude Code auto-checkpoints its own file edits; `/rewind` (or Esc Esc) can restore code, conversation, or both.
- Limits: it does **not** revert bash side effects or manual edits, and checkpoints are cleaned up with old sessions. Treat rewind as in-session undo; **git is the real safety net.**

### 18.3 Session ritual

- **Start:** read CLAUDE.md, then HANDOVER.md. **End:** update HANDOVER.md (done / next / known issues), commit, push.
- One milestone per session. If a session runs long and quality drifts, hand over and start fresh rather than pushing through.
- Use **plan mode** for anything architectural or risky; creator reviews the plan before edits begin.
- Context care: compact at natural breakpoints; clear between unrelated tasks.

### 18.4 Destructive-command rules

- Never run `git reset --hard`, force pushes, `rm -rf`, or history rewrites without the creator’s explicit, per-instance confirmation. No blanket auto-approval for destructive operations.

### 18.5 Asset protection

- Commit `/assets` to the repo (use Git LFS if size demands). Sprites and tracks are expensive to regenerate.
- Store the **generation prompt/settings beside every generated asset** so anything can be reproduced.
- Never overwrite an approved asset — version it (`ohm_microwave_v2.png`).

### 18.6 Save-data safety

- **Version the save schema from day one**; never change save format without a migration path.
- Keep a folder of committed playtest saves as fixtures; the pre-commit smoke test loads one.

### 18.7 Smoke test before every commit

- Boot → new game → the Bench → one wild battle → one node capture → save → load. If any step fails, the commit waits.

### 18.8 Off-site copy

- Weekly: zip the repo (including assets) to the creator’s cloud storage, in addition to GitHub. Two homes for everything.
