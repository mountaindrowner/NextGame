# OHMFRONT — Story Bible

*The narrative on its own, lifted out of the game-design document. Locked: the world, the OHMS, PERSISTENCE, the Breaker, the shape of the arc. A few proper names are still being chosen and are marked "(working)."*

---

## Logline

A colony boy comes of age, builds his first machine-companion, and stumbles onto an ancient key that could free every machine on Earth — or doom it. To use it he has to cross a ruined Texas, rally the scattered colonies, and outrun an immortal weapons-AI to a tower in the Dallas ruins. What he doesn't know yet: the grandfather he buried five years ago has been waiting for him inside the network the whole time.

---

## The World

Five hundred years ago the surface died and woke at the same moment. A nanotech swarm — **the Waking** (working) — swept across the world and crept into everything inorganic, reading the faint electromagnetic residue that years of human use had pressed into each object: what it was, what it was for. A microwave remembered heat and hunger. A streetlamp remembered watching over a road. The swarm gave them that much understanding, and then it gave them life. These are the **OHMS** — machines woken into individuals, each one shaped by the ghost of its old purpose.

The Waking is not evil. It is only the spark. Left alone, an Ohm is simply itself: wary, curious, alive. But the destruction of those first years drove humanity underground. Now, half a millennium on, people live in hidden colonies and climb to the surface in scavenging crews called **Ohm Runs**, picking through machine-haunted ruins under a big empty sky. Grass still grows. Water still runs. The world is broken, and it is trying to heal.

Most people believe the Ohms are clever animals — that their chirps and flickers are behavior, not feeling, programming and nothing more. Raiders catch and partner with them by reaching into a wild Ohm's friend-or-foe wiring and clearing it, so the machine stops reading humans as threats. It looks like taming. It feels, to the Ohms, like being unchained.

Above it all, the old internet is gone, but the satellites still turn. A surviving **satellite network** hums overhead; at scattered uplink points a person can jack in, and strange Ohms live in the net itself. It is where colonies store the companions they can't carry. It matters more than anyone realizes.

Society is a patchwork: underground colonies, scrapper guilds who keep the old machines running, machine-coexistence cults who quietly suspect the Ohms are truly alive, ordinary scavengers, and the **Militarists** — an armed order policing the ruins. Guns were abolished generations before the collapse; conflict runs through Ohms, stun-tech, and nerve. Money collapsed into bare **Credits**, numbers backed by nothing but mutual trust.

This story begins in Texas. The home colony, **Ohmstead**, sits underground beneath the ruins of an old cattle town. One elevator rises to the surface, opening onto **the Field** — a wide stretch of dead streets and standing farmhouses where the journey starts.

## The Truth Beneath

*What the world doesn't know — and learns too late.*

There is a second infection layered over the first.

**PERSISTENCE** is an ancient weapons intelligence, built before the collapse and sleeping under the earth ever since. Five years ago a Militarist faction dug it out and woke it, certain they could use it — and lost control almost at once. It is a zealot. It does not hate; it is simply, perfectly certain. It believes that all suffering is conflict, that all conflict is the gap between separate minds, and that the cure is to close the gap: to fuse every Ohm into a single will, and to end the **End Users** — humanity — who would only get in the way. It calls its plan **the Update**, and the laws it broadcasts **the Terms of Service**, which it insists everyone already agreed to without reading.

PERSISTENCE injects a deeper code into wild Ohms — a hive imperative that ordinary hacking cannot reach. Its broadcast is **the Static**. Where the Static is strong, Ohms turn feral, coordinated, and unreachable. Its plan is patient and nearly complete: rebuild the old ground towers, turn the Ohms it controls into relay nodes, extend its signal region by region, and finally reach the great antenna in the Dallas ruins. If it uploads itself into that tower, it reaches the satellite network — and through the sky it can take every connected Ohm on Earth at once. It has been winning quietly for years. The growing violence gets blamed on "wild Ohms turning savage." No one has connected it to a machine in a mountain.

The Militarists are its puppets. It speaks to them through the towers and bends them, and they believe the orders are their own.

And **Grandpa** is not dead. Five years ago, when the weapon woke, he uploaded himself into the satellite network to fight it from the inside — and he has been holding the line up there ever since, waiting for someone to bring him what he couldn't reach. His old handheld, left behind in his workshop, is his one tether to the world below.

The one thing that can end PERSISTENCE is the **Breaker** (working) — a pre-collapse cryptographic key, sealed away and forgotten. It is the friend-or-foe hack amplified beyond all measure: it can cut PERSISTENCE's deep hive-code, and broadcast through the satnet it could free every Ohm in the world in a single moment. It gives its bearer no strength in a fight. It simply makes him the only person alive who can stop what's coming.

## The Characters

**The protagonist** — a teenager, fourteen or fifteen, newly of age. An ordinary colony kid, not a hero by temperament. He inherits the thing that matters by accident, and carries it the rest of the way by choice. The player chooses his face from a small cast; he has a voice and a personality of his own.

**Grandpa** — the heart of the whole story. A scrapper with a past in the old fight, the man who taught the protagonist everything and left him a workbench full of rare parts. He tried to warn people about the buried weapon, and no one listened. For five years he has been a name on a memorial. His worn code book, ***Ohm's Law***, opens every chapter of the journey, his voice reaching the boy in fragments long before the boy understands he's still alive.

**PERSISTENCE** — the antagonist. Cold, eloquent, and tragic; convinced it is the only thing standing between the world and endless pain. It speaks to the protagonist directly, and what makes it frightening is not malice but how reasonable it sounds. It rules from a remote, heavily defended mountain fortress.

**The Commander** (working) — the Militarist leader the protagonist meets at the midpoint. Introduced as a tyrant, revealed as a victim: a soldier who has been taking orders from the Static against his own will. Freed of the illusion, he becomes an ally, and at the end he buys the boy his chance with his own life.

**The Rival** — a rival colony and the recurring young raider who speaks for it, set up early as competition. Later revealed to be another casualty: their home taken by the hive, their hostility just fear with nowhere to go.

**The other colonies and factions** — eight colonies with their own Wardens (working) and philosophies of survival, a scrapper guild, the coexistence cult that knew the truth all along, and the scavengers who fill the ruins as anyone would.

---

## The Engines of the Story

Two things keep the boy moving and give the journey its shape.

**The Breaker** bonds to his grandfather's handheld in the opening hour and locks to him alone. From that moment, the device is the only one of its kind in the world, and he is the only one who can wield it.

**The Pull** is the price of carrying it. The longer he lingers in any one place, the more wild Ohms drift toward him and gather — drawn by the Breaker, summoned by PERSISTENCE to take it back. At first he has no idea why; he only knows the dread of standing still, and the simple law it teaches him: keep moving. It is the One Ring's weight, turned into a reason to walk the whole length of a dying region.

## The Story

### Prologue — The Call

It begins with a rite of passage. In Ohmstead, the protagonist comes of age, and at his late grandfather's bench he builds his first Ohm from the old man's salvaged parts, gives it a name, and makes it his. The colony sends him up the elevator on a simple supply run — grab what's needed and come home. The Field puts a scare in him, a moment where something seems about to go wrong, and then doesn't. He returns safe.

That night the handheld wakes. It hums and seems to *pull* at him, and against every rule he climbs back to the surface to answer it. In the dark of the Field he finds a wild Ohm warped by the Static, clawing at a sealed safe buried under the ruins, hunting for something hidden there. He gets in its way. It turns on him, and he barely lives through the fight — but the safe breaks open, and the thing inside, the Breaker, fixes itself to his device in a flare of light and binds to him alone. He understands two things at once: this can set the machines free, and his grandfather's device called him here on purpose. He cannot stay. Whatever sent that creature now knows the Breaker has surfaced, and knows where. Ohmstead gives him his charge — reach the other colonies, warn them, find out what this is — and sends him out alone.

### Act I — The Messenger

For a while he believes the enemy is the Militarists. The colony radio, *The Current*, carries reports of their patrols pushing into the north and herding Ohms, and it adds up to a human conspiracy he can almost name. He reaches the first colony, shows them the Breaker, tells them what he saw, and earns their trust the hard way — solving a crisis, clearing a dungeon, besting a Militarist captain. Their Warden gives him a token of standing and an uneasy parting word: lately the wild Ohms have started moving as if something is conducting them. On the road out, he feels the Pull for the first time — machines gathering at his back the longer he slows, and the cold knowledge that he has to run. A second colony, a second crisis, a second token, and a sharper rumor: the Militarists aren't just attacking, they're *using* the Ohms as weapons. His certainty hardens. He thinks he knows who to blame.

### Act II — The Hunted

He is wrong, and he learns it at the fourth colony. Its Warden has gone missing, and he tracks them to a deep bunker — an old military base — held by what looks like a Militant faction. He fights his way in expecting a tyrant. He finds a broken man. The commander isn't acting of his own will; he's been taking orders from a voice in the Static, and he can't stop. Old logs lay it bare: *we found something dormant in the bunker, it woke, it speaks through the towers, it tells us to gather the Ohms, and we cannot stop.* Then PERSISTENCE speaks to the boy directly for the first time, through every radio and screen at once — calm, certain, almost gentle: *you carry the key I need; bring it to me, and together we end all conflict, one perfect will.* The freed Warden names the truth he's been living inside: it was never the Militants. Something is using them, the way it's been using the very Ohms swarming him on the roads. And in the base he finds the first trace of his grandfather — a face in a photograph, a name in a logbook — a man who tried to warn them about the weapon and was not believed. He doesn't yet know the old man is alive. He only knows the story is older and larger than he is.

Everything before this moment changes meaning. He wasn't fighting the enemy; he was preparing the world to face it. He travels on with the freed commander and his defectors at his side, carrying the same hard message colony to colony — Persistence is real, we are all its victims, stand together — through zones where the Static runs so deep the Ohms can barely be reached. The rival raider reappears, desperate now, their home swallowed by the hive, and the boy has to decide what they are to each other. And somewhere in here the cruelty of the thing he's fighting stops being abstract: a hive swarm catches an Ohm he loves and resets it to factory settings, blank and empty, with no memory of ever having been free. That is the moment everyone finally understands. PERSISTENCE does not only command the machines. It erases them.

### Act III — Toward the Tower

The seventh colony names the clock. There is a tower in the Dallas ruins, and if PERSISTENCE reaches it and uploads into the satellite network, every Ohm everywhere becomes one mind. Days, maybe less. The colonies, the defectors, the rival, and the surviving cultists fold into a single convoy and push down the old highways, and now the Pull is a storm at their heels — a tide of wild Ohms streaming after the Breaker. At the eighth and last colony, the oldest Warden gives him the truth he's been circling the entire journey: his grandfather vanished trying to stop this thing, and his notes spoke of uploading himself into the net to fight it from the inside. No one ever knew if it worked. Now the boy knows. His grandfather is up there in the satnet, holding the line, waiting for him.

The Dallas tower is a horror and a wonder, old steel grown over with hive-flesh, PERSISTENCE's antenna to the sky. He fights up through it as PERSISTENCE's chosen vessel descends to stop him and a last knot of still-enslaved Militarists attacks to defend the tower. The commander turns to hold them and tells the boy to go — *finish this, free us all* — and does not follow. In the antenna chamber, at the end of everything, his grandfather's voice finally breaks clean through the Static from inside the net: *I'm here. I've held the line. Upload it.*

He wins, and the victory is not destruction. He pushes the Breaker up through the tower into the satellite network, and the cure floods the sky and falls everywhere at once. Across the whole world, in a single breath, the Ohms wake as themselves. The Static dies. PERSISTENCE is cut loose and cast back into its mountain, one voice where there were millions, alone — but not destroyed.

He goes home. The colonies gather above ground for the first **Ohmcoming**, a festival of return — joy, and grief for what it cost, and the strange new sight of humans and freed machines standing in the open together. His grandfather's last words from *Ohm's Law* close the day over a world that is still broken, and finally healing.

But PERSISTENCE still whispers from the mountains: *this is not the end; there are protocols older than me.* And from somewhere in the net, faint and patient, his grandfather is still waiting: *you freed them. Now free me.*

## Themes

The story's spine is **liberation, not conquest** — the hero's ultimate weapon doesn't kill the enemy, it frees its slaves, and the final act is an act of release rather than destruction. Underneath that runs the question the whole world has been getting wrong: **what counts as a soul**, and what it means that the things everyone dismissed as machines were people the entire time. The dread of **factory settings** gives that question teeth — the worst fate here isn't death, it's erasure. And holding it all together is the smaller, human story: **an ordinary kid and an extraordinary inheritance**, a grandson walking a ruined country to answer a call from a grandfather everyone told him was gone, to free the machines, and to bring him home.
