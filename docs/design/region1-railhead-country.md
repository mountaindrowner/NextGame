## What this region is

**The near country.** The first ground the colonies took back, close enough
to Ohmstead for a day's Ohm Run and gentle enough for a kid's first one. Long
before the Waking it was cattle and grain country. The cattle and the grain
went north by rail to the city, and nobody remembers why it mattered.

**The machines still keep the old jobs.** Windmills pump for herds that are
gone. A grader keeps a road smooth that nobody drives. The grain tower lifts
empty buckets. The drive-in's projector lights a screen every night for an
audience of dead cars. These Ohms aren't hunting anyone. They're working.

**The one line:** *the work outlived the workers.*

That's the game's whole question, asked quietly before the story asks it out
loud. Early on it reads as quaint, machines stuck on a loop. After the reveal
it reads the other way: they were keeping faith with us the whole time.

**Mood:** the gentlest region in the game. Warm straw gold, faded barn red,
sun-bleached cedar, rusted tin, cottonwood shade along the water. Long
afternoon light. The danger here is small and human-sized. The wrongness
creeps in only at the northern edge.

## What's going on (the story here)

Act I, hours 0–8 (`docs/compendium/beats.md`): the Bench → the Field and the
first capture → the Breaker bonds → **the Spotting** (a Garrison patrol
decommissions three freed Ohms in the Field) → **Rook's raid** on an Ohmstead
cache → the Farm Road and the first feel of **the Pull** → **Railhead**, the
sabotaged relay, Captain Holt "off", the first Patch → north onto the Rust
Mile.

## Seen, not said: the underlayer

Seven threads run through the region. None is ever explained in dialogue
here. Each has a fixed visual language so a player can learn to read it.

| Thread | What you see | Where | What it means (later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Everything runs north.** | Fence lines, rails, telephone poles and the old roads all point the same way. From the top of the On-Ramp, on a clear day, a thin pin stands on the far horizon. | every section; the pin only at the On-Ramp | the city drew everything toward it once, and something there is drawing again. The pin is the Spire. |
| **2. The routines.** | Each section has one machine still doing its old job, animated in the background (table below). | every section | the Ohms carry the ghost of their purpose; it's the heart of the game. |
| **3. Grandpa was here.** | A small **brass tag** with three notches, the same mark that's on the Bench, fixed to machines someone kept running: the Pickett windmill, Railhead's switch-house, the Drive-In projector, the Grain Tower's lift motor. | four sections | Eli spent years out here keeping machines alive. It pays off in the Scrapper's Legacy thread. |
| **4. The Garrison is moving in.** | A **grey stencil**, a chevron with a tally number, on what they've "cleared": the three dead Ohm husks in the Field, a gate at the Stockyards, a checkpoint at the On-Ramp. More of it the farther north you go. | the Field, the Stockyards, the On-Ramp | the Garrison is following orders from the Static. |
| **5. Redbed passed through.** | **Red handprints** on a broken cache and a stripped truck. | Stockyard Lane, the Creek Road | Rook's raid; rivals living hard, not evil. |
| **6. The Static's first touch.** | Patches of **silver-grey grass** where wild Ohms twitch in time with each other; no birds; a faint hum in the sound. Only at the northern edge. | the Grain Line, the On-Ramp | PERSISTENCE's signal reaching south. |
| **7. People who stayed.** | The Pickett Place: a family living **topside**, in the open, with Ohms working beside them. | the Pickett Place | what the Ohmcoming looks like, already here, small. |

**Old-world rule:** nothing in this region names the old world. Billboards
are peeled blank, signs are worn to fragments, and the only words still
readable are the ones locals painted themselves.

## Shared kit (continuity)

Every section draws from the same kit, so the region reads as one place:
barbed-wire fence lines on leaning cedar posts · telephone poles · windmills
and stock tanks · tin sheds · cottonwoods along water, mesquite on dry ground,
dead snags · tumbleweeds · the rail line · round bales · faded red paint.

Each section adds **one signature** of its own. **Edges are natural:** a creek
bank, a bluff, a fence line with scrub behind it, a stand of trees. Never a
wall of trees on all four sides.

## People `[PROPOSAL]`

- **The Picketts** of the Pickett Place: **Ada Pickett** (the grandmother, the
  healer), **Hob Pickett** (her son, runs the shop), **Lettie** (his daughter,
  about ten, names every Ohm on the place). Ada knew Eli and won't say much.
- Each road gets 1–3 locals: a trainer or two, a traveler, a sign.

each place was: tools on Depot Road, farm machines on the Grain Line, cactus
