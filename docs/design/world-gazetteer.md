## The rule

The world is ~500 years past the Waking, far enough that **nothing resembles
itself**. It isn't Texas and doesn't map onto Texas. The land has moved, the
old names are gone, and people named what was left.

- A place is named for **what it does**, **what it did to people**, or **who
  took it**. (New Central: the Garrison took the old hub and declared it their
  capital.)
- It has to read well in plain text, with no voice actor to sell it. Plain words
  only, no puns, no attitude.
- It has to sound natural said out loud by a tired local: *"You're gonna have
  to go down the Rust Mile."* Real people name places like neighbors: after
  somebody, with "Old" for what outlasted everyone, or with plain land words
  (bend, fork, flats, gap, sinks).
- It must not name a real place, or land so close to one that a local would
  recognize it.

## How the land changed

- **The sea came in.** The old southeast coast drowned. A shallow salt sea now
  reaches far inland over sunk towns and marsh: **the Sinks**.
- **The big southern river died.** The river that once drew a border dried to a
  long, cracked bed full of wrecks. People call it **the Dryway**. Nobody
  crosses it, and nobody knows it was ever a river.
- **The dams broke.** Reservoirs drained into dead basins. Downstream, the main
  river backed up behind collapsed highway and drowned a forest: **the Drowned
  Woods**. The river itself, wide and brown and moving its bed every flood, is
  **Brownwater**.
- **The forest walked west.** The eastern woods spread over old farmland and
  swallowed the towns: **the Wildwoods**. Machine vines grow through them,
  called **wildwire**.
- **The west dried out and blew away.** Topsoil gone, mesas standing, dead wind
  farms to the horizon: **Sundog**. It ends at a broken ridge called **the
  Jawbone**.
- **Towns sank into hills.** Small towns collapsed under grass into low mounds.
  Locals call them **humps**: *"Every hump was a town once."*
- **Machines keep the old jobs.** A grader Ohm still cuts a road nobody uses; a
  sprinkler Ohm keeps one field green in a dead county. Those roads are **ghost
  roads**.

## Words people use

- **humps** (collapsed towns) · **ghost roads** (roads machines still keep) ·
  **topside** (the surface) · **the net** (the satnet; canon).
- **History, as people tell it** `[PROPOSAL]`: **the Before** → **the Waking**
  → **the Underyears** (the centuries underground) → **the Ohmcoming** (the
  end of this game).
- **Slurs for Ohms** (**LOCKED** words; who says which is `[PROPOSAL]`): people who lost things to Ohms talk about
  them this way. Each is built from OHMFRONT's own world. None echoes a
  real-world slur or comes from another work. The hero never uses them.

| Slur | Who says it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **appliance** | bitter colonists, old-timers | *"Don't name the appliance."* |
| **junk** | raiders, scavengers | *"Leave the junk, take the parts."* |
| **factory** | the Garrison | *"Keep it up and I'll send it back to factory."* |
| **statics** | everyone, for feral Ohms | *"Statics on the road tonight."* |
| **rattler** | ranch folk | *"Rattlers got into the feed again."* |
| **plug-lover** | anyone, for people who side with Ohms | Rook, early, at the hero. |

The mirror is already canon: PERSISTENCE calls humans **End Users**.

## Keep clear of

- **Real places and other works:** Fallout's *the Hub*, Lubbock's "Hub City",
  Trigun's *No Man's Land*; "clanker", "toaster", "skin job", "synth".
- **THE LONG NOON** (Mark's separate game, South Texas): its *Glass, Fork,
  Span, Choir, Bleach, Gate, Dead River* stay out of OHMFRONT. Shared today:
  *Cistern, Nursery, Warden, Keeper, Hale*. If they're ever made distinct,
  rename on THE LONG NOON's side.
